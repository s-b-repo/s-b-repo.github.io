// facts · an honest take

The Current Cyber Struggle.

The security industry has a credibility problem — and most of it is self-inflicted. Below are the structural failures we see every week: the ones that drain budgets, burn out talent, and leave organisations compliant but wide open. No vendor spin. Just what's actually broken.

Current cyber struggle

Compliance Theater

Compliance ≠ Security

ISO audits are bureaucracy, not testing. Audits like ISO 27001 are run as paperwork exercises by assessors who never touch the systems. Teams burn weeks generating documents, productivity tanks, and the actual attack surface stays untested. A passed audit is a filing cabinet — not a secure network.

Paper Tigers

Cert Inflation

Certifications don't make you competent. The field treats certs as proof of skill. They aren't. Exam dumps let anyone memorise their way to a badge without ever finding a real bug, reading real code, or owning a real box.

Broken Pipeline

Gatekeeping

"Entry-level" that demands 5 years. Junior listings requiring senior experience, a stack of certs, and a degree — for a salary that assumes none of it. Impossible bars that lock capable people out while the industry cries "skills shortage".

Process Over Proof

Broken Hiring

Hiring that never tests the skill. ATS keyword filters and trivia interviews decide who gets in. Almost nobody asks a candidate to read code, find a vulnerability, or break something live.

Scanner Dependence

Tool Theater

Buying logos instead of doing the work. Budgets go to brand-name dashboards and a "we ran a scan" checkbox. Automated scanners miss exactly the logic flaws, chained bugs and custom weaknesses real attackers walk through.

Proof Loses to Paperwork

Talent Ignored

Demonstrated ability — shipped tools, real findings, public research — counts for less than a degree and the right alphabet soup. The industry filters for credentials over capability, then wonders why it keeps missing the people who can actually defend it.
