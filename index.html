// case study

Proven results in the real world.

Public Sector · 9-Day Engagement 01

Full-scope penetration test — external, internal, wireless, physical

Full-scope penetration test of a public-sector organisation. Found 36 vulnerabilities — 18 critical, including a collaboration platform running in production more than eight years past end-of-life. Retrieved national identity numbers and personal records from an unsecured server — a reportable personal-information breach under POPIA.

Achieved domain admin by exploiting a long-patched SMB vulnerability on a forgotten legacy Windows host, and extracted 5 staff credentials. Bypassed physical access control at reception and reached a restricted office undetected. The wireless network used obsolete encryption alongside an open guest SSID. DMARC was monitoring-only — outbound mail was fully spoofable. Not one alert was raised.

  • 36 vulnerabilities — 18 critical, 9 high
  • 5 confirmed breaches exploited on-site
  • Citizen PII extracted — POPIA triggered
  • Physical + wireless + internal + external
Service Provider · Gap Assessment 02

57 hosts. 50+ exposed VM images. 7 downstream orgs affected.

White-box penetration test across 3 subnets of an internet and managed service provider. Extracted 27 employee password hashes from 10 unique accounts across 8 mounted VMs — all sharing an identical hash. Pulled a complete perimeter firewall configuration including 17 encrypted credentials, a customer's directory bind account, 9 VPN users, 3 RADIUS secrets, and 5 SSL private keys.

Discovered 50+ virtual disk images on unauthenticated NFS shares — complete backup repositories including a multi-terabyte corporate mail database. Mapped 4 Active Directory domains with 21 Windows hosts — all enumerated without credentials. The exposure chain cascaded to 7 downstream organisations across the public sector, transport, manufacturing and financial services.

  • 37 vulns — 12 critical, 11 high
  • 27 hashes extracted from 10 accounts
  • 4 AD domains mapped, 21 hosts, zero creds
  • 7 downstream organizations secured

// common questions

Questions we get asked first.

What services does Cyber Sec offer?

Six focused service lines: custom pentesting tools, vulnerability research and exploit development, code audits, open-source security audits, vendor product security testing, and detection engineering and SOC build-out. Every engagement is scoped and authorized in writing before any work begins.

Does Cyber Sec do vulnerability research and exploit development?

Yes. Original research — zero-day discovery, reverse engineering, and protocol analysis — plus reproducible proof-of-concept exploits against the software you build or depend on, with responsible disclosure where third parties are involved.

Can Cyber Sec build our detection and SOC monitoring stack?

Yes, on a project basis. We design, build, tune, and hand over your monitoring stack — Wazuh or Elastic SIEM, log-ingestion pipelines, and DFIR automation fitted to your environment. It is built, documented, and yours to run — not a managed subscription.

Who does Cyber Sec work with?

Software teams, product vendors, and organisations that need hands-on offensive security and detection engineering. Every engagement is scoped to your specific stack and threat model.

How does the code audit process work?

Code audits are manual, line-level security reviews — not automated scans. We identify vulnerabilities, logic flaws, and supply-chain risks, then deliver prioritized findings with business impact analysis and concrete remediation steps. Re-testing after fixes are applied is included.

Can I see your work before hiring you?

Yes — that is the point of the section above. Over 300 public tools and research projects, including Rustsploit, clean-room reverse engineering of an enterprise SSL VPN client, independent I2P and gSOAP proof-of-concept exploits, and SOC tooling.
