Responsible Disclosure.

Found something in ours? Here is how to tell us, what protection you have while doing it, and what we commit to in return.

Last updated 3 August 2026

We test other people's systems for a living, so it would be poor form to make it hard to report a problem in ours. This policy sets out what we consider in scope, what we ask you not to do, and what we commit to in return.

Report to

1. Safe harbour

If you make a good-faith effort to comply with this policy while researching a vulnerability in an asset listed in scope below, we will:

  • not pursue or support civil action against you;
  • not report you to law enforcement, and will make clear that your actions were authorised under this policy if a third party does;
  • treat your research as authorised access for the purposes of the Cybercrimes Act 19 of 2020 and section 86 of the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act 25 of 2002; and
  • work with you on disclosure rather than against you.

This authorisation is limited to systems we actually control. We cannot grant safe harbour over anyone else's infrastructure — including our hosting and CDN providers, whose own policies apply to them.

2. In scope

  • cybersec.org.za and its subdomains
  • Public source repositories under github.com/s-b-repo, including vulnerabilities in the tooling itself
  • Our published security.txt, DNS configuration and email authentication records (SPF, DKIM, DMARC)

3. Out of scope

  • Client systems. Never test a system because you saw it mentioned here. Our clients have not authorised you, and we cannot authorise you on their behalf.
  • Third-party infrastructure we merely use: GitHub Pages, Cloudflare, Proton. Report those to the provider directly.
  • Denial of service, volumetric or stress testing of any kind.
  • Social engineering, phishing or physical intrusion against us or anyone associated with us.
  • Reports produced solely by running an automated scanner, with no demonstrated impact.
  • Missing hardening headers, weak TLS ciphers or similar configuration observations with no exploitable consequence. Tell us anyway if you like, but they are not vulnerabilities.
  • Vulnerabilities requiring a rooted or physically compromised device, or an already-compromised browser.

4. What we ask

  • Give us a reproducible proof of concept: the exact request, input and version.
  • Use only accounts and data you own or that we have provided.
  • Do not access, modify, delete or exfiltrate anyone else's data. If you encounter personal information, stop, do not save a copy, and tell us what you saw in general terms.
  • Do not degrade the service. Rate-limit yourself.
  • Do not leave persistence — no backdoors, no test accounts, no uploaded files. If you must create something to demonstrate the issue, tell us so we can remove it.
  • Give us a reasonable window to fix the issue before publishing.

5. What we commit to

StageTarget
Acknowledge your report2 business days
Initial assessment and triage5 business days
Fix or documented mitigation plan30 days for a confirmed issue
Coordinated public disclosure90 days, or sooner by agreement

We will keep you informed, tell you honestly if we disagree that something is a vulnerability and why, and credit you by name or handle when the fix ships — unless you would rather we did not.

6. No bounty

We do not operate a paid bug bounty. This is a disclosure policy, not a commercial programme. What we offer is a fast, technically competent response from the person who wrote the code, public credit, and a genuine fix.

7. Vulnerabilities we find in your software

The same standard runs the other way. When our own research turns up a vulnerability in third-party software, we disclose it privately to the vendor first, allow a reasonable remediation period before any publication, never sell findings to a broker, and publish proof of concept code only once a fix is available or the vendor has clearly declined to act. Where an engagement uncovers an issue in a product a client depends on, we write the report in a form the client can hand to the vendor directly.
