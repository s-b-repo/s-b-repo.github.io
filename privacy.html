1. The Information Officer

POPIA defines the information officer of a private body by reference to the "head of a private body" in section 1 of PAIA. For a company that is the chief executive officer or equivalent officer, or the person acting as such. Cyber Sec has a single director, who is therefore the Information Officer by operation of law rather than by appointment.

Section 56 of POPIA requires a responsible party to designate deputy information officers only so far as is necessary to make the body as accessible as reasonably possible for requests. A one-person practice with a single published contact address is already as accessible as it can be, so no deputies are designated.

The Information Officer is required by section 55 and Regulation 4 to register with the Information Regulator before taking up the duties, and is responsible for the compliance framework, the personal information impact assessment, the PAIA manual, dealing with requests, and liaising with the Regulator.

2. The short version

This website is deliberately built to collect almost nothing.

  • The forms do not send us anything directly. Every form on this site opens your own email client with a pre-filled message. Nothing is transmitted to us, or to any third party, until you press send in your own mail application. We receive your message the same way we would receive any other email.
  • We set no cookies. None. The only thing stored in your browser is a currency preference (cs-ccy) in local storage, and only if you change the currency selector on the store page yourself. It contains a three-letter currency code and nothing else.
  • We run no advertising, profiling or cross-site tracking of any kind.

3. What we process, and why

3.1 Visiting the website

The site is hosted on a static-site hosting provider and served through Cloudflare. Both necessarily process your IP address and request metadata in order to deliver the page to you and to protect the service. We also use Cloudflare Web Analytics, which is cookieless and does not fingerprint or track individuals across sites; it gives us aggregate page-view counts only.

Lawful basis: our legitimate interest in operating and securing the site (POPIA section 11(1)(f)).

3.2 Contacting us

If you use a form, you choose what to include. The fields offered are your name, company, email address, telephone number, engagement type, how you heard about us, and your message. As explained above, this composes an email in your own mail client — we only receive it if you send it. Once received, it sits in our mailbox like any other correspondence.

Lawful basis: steps taken at your request prior to entering a contract (section 11(1)(b)), and our legitimate interest in responding to enquiries (section 11(1)(f)).

Providing this information is entirely voluntary. If you provide less, we may not be able to scope or price an engagement, but there is no other consequence.

3.3 If we later enable a form backend

The site supports posting form submissions to Formspree or Web3Forms instead of composing an email. This is not currently enabled — no endpoint is configured, and no submission is transmitted to either provider. If we enable it, that provider becomes an operator under POPIA, this notice will be updated before the change goes live, and the form itself will say where the data is going.

3.4 During an engagement

This is the part that matters most, and it is specific to what we do. A penetration test, code audit or detection engineering build routinely exposes us to personal information belonging to your staff, customers or users — credentials, email addresses, identity numbers, database contents, log data. In relation to that information:

  • You remain the responsible party. We act as your operator under POPIA sections 20 and 21, processing only on your documented instruction.
  • Every engagement is governed by a written agreement covering scope, authorisation, confidentiality and the handling and destruction of evidence.
  • We collect the minimum needed to demonstrate a finding. Where a proof of concept can be shown with a redacted or synthetic record, we use one.
  • Evidence is stored encrypted at rest, and destroyed at the end of the agreed retention period or on your written instruction, whichever is sooner.
  • If we discover a breach affecting your data subjects, we notify you without undue delay so that you can meet your own section 22 obligations.

4. Special personal information and children

We do not seek special personal information as defined in POPIA section 26, and we do not knowingly process the personal information of children (section 34). If such information reaches us incidentally during an engagement, it is handled under the engagement agreement and destroyed as soon as it is no longer needed to evidence a finding.

6. Transfers outside South Africa

Because the site is hosted and delivered by the providers above, personal information is processed outside the Republic. POPIA section 72 permits this where the recipient is subject to a law, binding agreement or corporate rules that provide an adequate level of protection. We rely on the data-processing terms of each provider, which impose comparable obligations. Switzerland, where our email is hosted, is recognised as providing an adequate level of data protection.

7. How long we keep it

  • Enquiries that do not become engagements: up to 24 months, then deleted.
  • Engagement records and reports: for the period agreed in the engagement agreement, and in any event no longer than required by law.
  • Evidence and artefacts gathered during testing: destroyed at the end of the agreed retention period or on your written instruction.
  • Accounting records: retained as required by the Companies Act and tax legislation.
  • Server and CDN logs: retained by the providers per their own schedules.

8. How we protect it

POPIA section 19 requires appropriate, reasonable technical and organisational measures. Ours include encryption in transit (HTTPS is enforced site-wide) and at rest, a Content-Security-Policy on every page, self-hosted fonts so that no third-party font provider sees your IP address, multi-factor authentication on our accounts, least-privilege access, and encrypted storage for engagement evidence.

No safeguard is absolute. If a security compromise affects your personal information, we will notify you and the Information Regulator as required by section 22.

9. Your rights

Under POPIA sections 5, 23, 24 and 25 you may:

  • ask whether we hold personal information about you, and request a copy of it;
  • request that we correct or delete information that is inaccurate, irrelevant, excessive, out of date, incomplete, misleading or unlawfully obtained;
  • object, on reasonable grounds, to our processing (Regulation Form 1);
  • request destruction or deletion of a record (Regulation Form 2);
  • not be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing — we make no such decisions;
  • complain to the Information Regulator; and
  • institute civil proceedings under section 99.

Email stephanbotesIT@proton.me to exercise any of these. We will respond within a reasonable period, and in any event within the periods prescribed by PAIA where a formal access request is made. We may ask you to verify your identity first, so that we do not disclose your information to someone else. Access requests follow the procedure in our PAIA manual. A prescribed fee may apply to access requests; correction, objection and deletion are free.

10. Direct marketing

POPIA section 69 restricts unsolicited electronic direct marketing. We do not send it. The "Stay Informed" form on the resources page composes an email from your own client, which is an express opt-in by you. Every message we send in reliance on it will offer a way to stop receiving them, and asking us to stop is enough.
